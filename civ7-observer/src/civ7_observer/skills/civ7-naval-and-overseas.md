# Naval operations and overseas development

**Use when:** Use for coastal defense, ocean reconnaissance, overseas settlement, amphibious attacks or transporting valuable cargo and civilians.

Needs to read: commanders, context, economy, map, military, production, resources, rules, trade. Needs to execute (only if enabled): commanders.deploy, settlements.found, trade.deliver, units.attack, units.move. Rules: R-GEOGRAPHY, R-NAVAL, R-TRADE.

## Required situation

Obtain each vessel’s current class, vision, movement and attack mode, embark/ocean-access rules, safe known routes, transport/Commander state, disclosed regional classifications, viable destination/landing sites and cargo generation/delivery prerequisites. Map appearance alone does not establish Homeland or Distant Lands classification.

## Procedure

1. Define the expedition’s economic or military objective. A new colony, a trade connection, a protected convoy corridor and a siege landing need different assets.
2. Build a complete route plan. Check departure, ocean access, scouting, staging, safe landing or delivery destination and return/continuation. Budget the shore-side infrastructure and defense as well as the ships.
3. Use roles deliberately: scouting/vision, screening, damage and transport. Read current-age ship behavior; do not send every vessel into melee because an Antiquity example did so.
4. Send reconnaissance before fragile cargo where uncertainty is material. Prefer shorter staged movements near hostile coasts, and reassess when new units appear. An escort that cannot keep pace or protect the vulnerable endpoint is not a sufficient escort.
5. Establish a defensible foothold before committing a second wave of civilians or a large unsupported army. A high-yield colony that cannot connect, deliver or survive may lose to a modest secure alternative.
6. For an amphibious attack, confirm legal landing tiles, movement/action costs and post-landing protection. Do not count an embarked unit as an immediately ready land attacker without native confirmation.
7. For a cargo plan, monitor generation prerequisites, actual cargo/value, escort readiness and eligible delivery. Confirm the delivered result; do not treat spawned cargo as collected income or score.

## When not to go overseas

Do not force an ocean plan on every map. Existing land access, inland threats, a poor route or a faster domestic victory path can make naval investment a distraction. Use the cheapest sufficient naval capability for the selected goal, and retain a fallback when a choke is blocked.

Against hostile or potentially hostile navies, a route’s remembered safety expires. Scout again before dispatching expensive civilians. Do not invent a hidden submarine or assume one is absent; use only legally available detection and explicit uncertainty.
