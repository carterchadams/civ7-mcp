# Triumph selection and Dedication value

**Use when:** Use when choosing optional Age objectives, evaluating a first-to race, claiming a reward or selecting next-Age Dedications.

Needs to read: context, decisions, economy, military, production, rules, triumphs, victory. Needs to execute (only if enabled): dedications.choose, triumphs.claim. Rules: R-TRIUMPH, R-VICTORY.

## Required situation

Read the active Triumph set, enabled objectives, type, progress, remaining conditions, first-to status, known availability, exact rewards and selectable Dedications. A static list of all possible Triumphs is not the live contest. Include the Age horizon and existing strategic commitments.

## Procedure

1. Identify objectives already close to completion through normal useful play. These often deserve attention because their incremental cost is low.
2. For a detour, calculate the extra actions, production, research, risk and time beyond the existing plan. Compare the reward to that incremental cost—not the emotional appeal of finishing a checklist.
3. Check contestability. A first-to reward already claimed by another player is not a reason to keep spending for that reward. Unknown rival progress is uncertain, not evidence of an empty race.
4. Distinguish immediate payoff from next-Age value. Evaluate a Dedication against the empire that will actually use it and the available selection capacity. Do not assume every unlocked reward can be activated.
5. Prioritize a small compatible portfolio. One or two well-aligned side goals is a useful starting heuristic; more are justified only when they are nearly free or clearly improve the finish.
6. At a reward/selection screen, read the actual options and resolve it explicitly. Record the activated effect and duration rather than only “Triumph complete.”
7. Abandon a now-poor objective when a threat, timing change or rival completion destroys its value. Sunk cost does not rescue an unachievable deadline.

## Strategic trade-offs

A side objective can be excellent because it pays for something you wanted anyway. It can also be a trap when it forces a war, an irrelevant research chain or infrastructure that will not finish in time. Compare it with the victory monitor before committing. Modern rewards may affect an immediate finish, so their urgency can be higher than the same-looking optional objective earlier.

Do not infer a guaranteed number of turns until the Age ends from one progress percentage. If the remaining window is uncertain, use a conservative estimate and mark the confidence. Avoid desperate irreversible expenditures based on an exact deadline the game has not actually disclosed.
