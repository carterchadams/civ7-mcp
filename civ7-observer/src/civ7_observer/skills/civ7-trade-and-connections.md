# Trade routes and settlement connections

**Use when:** Use when connecting settlements, choosing a merchant destination, improving trade access, or diagnosing missing resource and Food benefits.

Needs to read: context, diplomacy, economy, map, military, resources, rules, trade. Needs to execute (only if enabled): diplomacy.propose, trade.connect, trade.establish, units.move. Rules: R-GEOGRAPHY, R-TOWN, R-TRADE.

## Required situation

Obtain the actual settlement connection graph, merchant roster, active routes, eligible targets, route limits/costs, incoming resources, travel paths and public diplomatic conditions. Connections and foreign trade agreements are related but not interchangeable; ask which specific relation supplies the desired benefit.

## Procedure

1. Start from an economic problem: a support Town’s output is not reaching a City, a useful resource cannot be assigned, or a foreign partner offers a valuable import. Trace the missing edge or eligibility condition before purchasing another merchant.
2. Compare the complete route package: travel time, setup cost, resource/economic benefit, maintenance or diplomatic requirement, civilian exposure, and likely duration. The closest partner is not necessarily the best.
3. Check native eligibility. Mountains, coasts, range, borders and required infrastructure can matter; visual nearness or a drawn road should not substitute for the connection result.
4. Look for a modest connection that improves several parts of the empire at once. A bridge settlement or link may be worthwhile, but count its total establishment and defense cost rather than treating it as free infrastructure.
5. Before increasing trade capacity, confirm there is an additional worthwhile legal route ready to use it. Reserve Influence against the real quoted cost and other commitments.
6. Give merchants a safe approach and an explicit destination. Revalidate after a war, border change, new visible threat or another route consumes capacity.
7. Confirm the route and its resulting resource/connection effects. Record which settlements and assignments depend on it. If disrupted, reallocate scarce resources deliberately rather than preserving a now-impossible economy in the plan.

## Strategic balance

Trade can build relationships as well as the economy, but dependency on a threatened partner has a cost. Avoid concentrating every critical import on a route likely to disappear during the very war you are preparing. Conversely, do not reject safe mutually beneficial trade simply because the other side also gains; compare how those gains affect the actual victory race.

A City resource need not require converting the Town that happens to own its source. Ask where the game permits assignment through the existing network. Fixing connectivity or selecting another eligible City may be cheaper than adding a new City.
