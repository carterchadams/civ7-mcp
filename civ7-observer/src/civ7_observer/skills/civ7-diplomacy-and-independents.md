# Diplomacy, Independent Powers and war support

**Use when:** Use for first contacts, incoming proposals, Independent Power investment, treaties, war preparation or peace negotiation.

Needs to read: context, decisions, diplomacy, economy, map, military, rules, trade, victory. Needs to execute (only if enabled): diplomacy.propose, diplomacy.respond, independents.act. Rules: R-DIPLOMACY.

## Required situation

Read only disclosed contacts, relations, active agreements and timers, relevant agendas/effects, current Influence, legal proposal/response options and costs, public war status, Independent Power progress and visible competing interests. Undisclosed foreign intentions remain uncertain, especially against a human.

## Procedure

1. Classify the relationship’s actual strategic value: safe border, useful trade partner, shared threat, independent bonus, dangerous rival or near-term victory leader. Do not confuse friendliness with a permanent guarantee or hostility with an obligation to attack.
2. For an incoming offer, compare accept, support and reject as separate priced options. Include what the other side gains and whether that benefit accelerates an imminent rival finish. Do not reflexively pay for support or reject beneficial cooperation to spite an opponent.
3. Reserve Influence for known important responses, defense/war support or an ongoing contest. Evaluate a new agreement by total effect over its actual duration, not by one turn of yield or a launch-era fixed cost.
4. Before investing in an Independent Power, name the desired benefit and compare it with direct development or diplomacy elsewhere. Consider proximity, defense, visible competition, progress remaining and expected usable duration. Do not disperse or attack a potential useful partner without comparing the peaceful option.
5. Before war, inspect alliances, access, exposed trade links and support/war-weariness consequences. Include those effects in the force plan rather than discovering them after committing.
6. Negotiate peace when the marginal benefit of continued fighting is lower than attrition and economic damage, or when peace enables a faster finish. Evaluate actual settlement, Gold, Influence and other offered terms. Preserve what the empire can sustain; do not automatically demand or accept everything.
7. Track expirations and response deadlines in the turn plan. Recheck after an Age transition rather than assuming the old agreement is still active.

## Human-opponent caution

Agendas and relationship stages are useful disclosed game mechanics, not a mind-reading model. Against another agent or a human, assess observable military positions, offered terms and incentives. A stated promise is uncertain future behavior; record it separately from binding game-enforced treaty effects.
