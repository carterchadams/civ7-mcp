# Opening priorities and expansion tempo

**Use when:** Use in the first development phase or when deciding whether the empire should expand, stabilize or invest in existing settlements.

Needs to read: context, decisions, economy, map, military, production, rules. Needs to execute (only if enabled): production.choose, settlements.found, units.move. Rules: R-CIV, R-GROWTH, R-TOWN.

## Required situation

Read the starting unit roster, legal founding options, nearby visible yields and approaches, leader/civilization effects, available production, growth and research choices, settlement headroom and visible threats. Distinguish a late-Age or advanced start from an ordinary opening.

## Conditional opening policy

Establish a useful initial settlement promptly unless a nearby, demonstrably better legal site justifies the delay. Do not spend indefinite turns chasing a perfect unseen location. Obtain enough local reconnaissance to choose expansion corridors and enough defense to protect what you are building. Develop Food and Production while initiating both research tracks when choices become available.

Do not copy a fixed “Scout → X → Y” queue. Select the next investment by its binding problem:

| Evidence | Candidate response |
|---|---|
| Little local knowledge, no immediate visible danger | A scouting capability or a safe scouting move. |
| A credible hostile force can reach the settlement/civilian | Defender, safer position, fortification or feasible diplomacy. |
| A strong known site, sustainable headroom and safe access | Prepare and protect a settler; do not postpone expansion for every attractive building. |
| Growth is constrained while useful tiles remain | A net-positive Food/growth intervention. |
| Plans are slow because the production base is weak | An improvement or building whose actual Production payoff arrives soon enough. |
| No acute constraint and a strong usable unlock is near | Investment supporting the chosen strategic direction. |

## Expansion test

Before committing a settler, name its destination or a small ranked set of destinations. Budget the unit, escort, travel time, establishment needs and effect on empire-wide stability. Include settlements likely to arrive through conquest or integration when assessing headroom. A legal new settlement can still be an economic mistake.

Compare expansion with developing an existing site. Additional land and resource access are useful only if the empire can hold and exploit them. Conversely, avoiding all expansion because the capital has unfinished buildings can leave the empire without enough long-term capacity. Re-evaluate after each settlement, not only after filling the nominal limit.

## Anti-ignorance checks

Do not plan around nonexistent builder actions. Do not assume every settlement must be a City. Do not assume an exposed settler can safely outrun an unobserved threat. Do not chase every Wonder or every optional objective in the opening. Never allow the selected theme—science, culture or military—to eliminate basic defense and development.
