# Force planning, recruitment and operational readiness

**Use when:** Use before war, when a frontier becomes unsafe, when recruiting reinforcements, or when deciding whether military spending is sufficient.

Needs to read: commanders, context, diplomacy, economy, map, military, production, rules, victory. Needs to execute (only if enabled): production.choose, production.purchase, units.move, units.upgrade. Rules: R-COMBAT, R-COMMAND, R-DIPLOMACY.

## Required situation

Read own ready and arriving forces, health, unit roles, available recruitment/upgrades, movement routes, defensible positions, exposed settlements/civilians, public enemy sightings and diplomatic obligations. Represent enemy strength as a lower-bound observation plus uncertainty, not an exact invented army.

## Procedure

1. Define an operation with a bounded objective: hold a passage, protect a settler, relieve a siege, capture one valuable settlement, or interrupt a specific victory condition. “Go to war and see what happens” is not a plan.
2. Estimate required functions rather than just unit count: frontline protection, damage against units, damage against defenses, a legal capture capability, reconnaissance and delivery. Add naval or air roles only when the operation actually needs them.
3. Compare arrival time with the objective’s deadline. A powerful unit completing far away is not present defense. Use existing forces, upgrades or purchases when their timing is decisive and funded.
4. Budget the whole operation: recruitment, upgrades, maintenance, losses, reinforcement, diplomatic costs and the economy left at home. Keep an actual reserve for plausible setbacks.
5. Stage before committing. Verify access, deployment space, a protected Commander/support structure and a retreat route. Avoid sending newly produced units forward one by one into a concentrated opponent.
6. Set explicit go/no-go and stop conditions: adequate ready force, acceptable predicted exchange, target access, enemy reinforcement or failure to progress. Re-evaluate when reconnaissance contradicts the estimate.
7. After success, choose hold, consolidate, negotiate or pursue the next objective. Replenish before extending an exhausted army merely because it won one battle.

## Efficient defense

Defense is a means to preserve development, not a mandate for infinite recruitment. A well-positioned modest force or effective diplomacy can free more economy than matching every possible hidden threat. On the other hand, don't price a frontier's safety as zero merely because the current observation omits foreign units.

Recruitment should close a role gap. Another ranged unit is less helpful when the force lacks a safe frontline or legal capture unit. An upgraded core can be more valuable than many obsolete units, but check actual costs, locations and whether upgrading consumes an action opportunity.
